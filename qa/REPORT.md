Scores are the reviewers' own lens scores, averaged where two reviewers scored the same category and rounded to the nearest 0.5.

**What the site is today.** Velocity works end to end for exactly one person: the owner, training for IRONMAN Texas, whose plan, paces, heart-rate zones, race legs, race-week story, Texas heat advice and example coach conversation are written into the code. For that athlete it is a competent, if visually generic, dashboard whose arithmetic mostly adds up. For anyone else — a 2:45 marathoner, a 380-W cyclist, a 70.3 athlete in Dubai — it hands back the owner's numbers as their own, and in several places shows figures that are simply wrong (849 g of fat on race day, a 3-minute run where 30 was typed, a plan that says 14 hours and schedules 11). Three structural causes sit behind most of the 453 findings. **(1) One athlete's personal constants are baked in everywhere:** 155 bpm as everyone's threshold, the owner's pace and HR zones as everyone's targets, Ironman legs on every race card, "Texas" and "Munich" in the nutrition guide, the seed coach thread on every new account — about 45 rows below trace to this. **(2) No design-token discipline:** 34 font sizes, 29 backgrounds, 17 radii, 17 shadows, 13 control heights and 31 card paddings, each file inventing its own values, which is what makes the product read as generated rather than designed. **(3) Components that cannot shrink on a phone:** fixed-width inputs, non-wrapping tab rows, seven-column tables and `1fr` grids with intrinsic minimums push 36 of 54 phone states past the 390-px screen. Fixing these three causes (work packages 1, 3 and 4 in section 6) closes more than half of the list.

## 4. Nutrition model

This is the one-page version of the specification in the appendix of `qa/findings/NUT.md` (§0–§K, with sources). It replaces `sessionFuel`, `sessionKcal`, the carb/fluid/sodium parts of `targetsFor` and the Carbs-per-hour calculator with one model. Internal units kg, g, ml, kcal, minutes; display follows the athlete's unit setting. Rules marked **thin** in NUT.md are judgement calls rather than measured consensus.

**Inputs.** Existing and mostly unused: `profile.weight_kg, height_cm, birth_year, sex, goal, weight_stages, base_kcal, bottle_ml`; `Session.sport, min, intensity, start, date, status, actual`; `Activity.min, hr, mi, pace_s, mph, yd, exertion`; `intake.fitness.bike_ftp, lthr, vo2max, run_pace, swim_pace_100`; `athlete.race.{kind, date, city}`; `plan.phase`. New at set-up (§K below): `body_fat_pct`, `sweat_tests[]`, `sweat_na`, `gut`, `caffeine_habit`, `caffeine_race`, `diet`, `restrictions[]`, `products[]`. New per session: `fuel_g_h` (stamped by the plan), `hot`, `segments[]`. New per log entry: `at` (HH:MM) and `fuel: true`. Derived: `FFM = weight × (1 − body_fat/100)` (default body fat M 15 % / F 23 %, shown "estimated"); `RMR` = Mifflin–St Jeor, or Cunningham `500 + 22 × FFM` when body fat is entered. **Session class:** `micro` = under 45 min and easy, or strength, or Technique; `key` = ≥ 90 min or Tempo/Intervals/Race; `easy` = the rest. Intensity groups: easy = Zone 2/Aerobic/Technique · moderate = Endurance · hard = Tempo/Intervals · race = Race.

## 10. Challenges

The reviewers' objections to the final decisions, with evidence. No new ones added.

1. **Decision 5 (phase/week position instead of a race countdown) — A2.** Every marathon plan a runner has used (Pfitzinger, Hansons, Daniels) is indexed by weeks-to-go, and the plan's own copy says "four weeks out" and "three-week taper". Proposal: show both facts without countdown theatre — "Base 1 · Week 1 of 14 · 13 weeks to Houston".
2. **Decision 6 (sessions, not days) with the current matching — A2.** Eight runs logged in week 1 count as "4 of 5 sessions" because Monday and Tuesday were rest days (s3-steps.md 50); a runner who moves his long run to Monday is told he skipped it. Proposal, adopted into V-016: match a logged activity to the nearest unfilled planned session of the same sport within ± 1 day.
3. **Decision 3 (no readiness or recovery score) vs the Health score — C2.** `analysis.ts:141` scores 7-day RHR and HRV against targets; that is the WHOOP/Garmin readiness construction under another name, and today it shows 73 from one stale VO2 reading (C2-06). Either delete it (adopted, V-025) or accept that the product has a recovery score and build it properly (C2-22).
4. **Decision 7 (the drawn Garmin-style watch) — D3.** The drawing carries the word "GARMIN" (`Watch.tsx:46`) and Garmin's dial styling on a product that also sells Garmin devices through affiliate links, next to a false "Connected" label (V-050). Keep the drawing if the owner wants it, but remove the wordmark text and never pair it with a connection status.
5. **Decision 8 (the red feedback button) — D3.** On every screenshot it is the only saturated red in the product and sits 24 px from the wordmark, so the brand's first impression is an error colour. If it must stay red, use the semantic `--danger` at 32 px with no second "BETA" pill (adopted, V-157); the better execution is an accent ghost button next to the avatar.

Of the known-missing integrations, the reviewers agree Garmin automatic sync matters most: without it every number after 28 September is a planned number, and the training-plus-nutrition link (V-032) cannot read what the athlete did.
